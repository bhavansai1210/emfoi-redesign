# Public SEO Evidence Log — EMFOI US Market

**Research date:** August 15, 2026  
**Data basis:** Public pages and search results only. No paid Ahrefs, Semrush, Similarweb, DataForSEO, GA4, or Search Console data is available. This log must not be used to infer search volumes, rankings, traffic, keyword difficulty, backlink quality, or causality.

## Source 1 — EMFOI legacy public site

URL: https://www.emfoi.com/  
Observed on: August 15, 2026

The legacy homepage identifies EMFOI broadly as an IT-services provider. Its visible service navigation includes UI/UX services, IT consulting and advisory, web development, mobile development, AI/ML development, cybersecurity, network connectivity, ERP solutions, and managed services. Its visible industry navigation includes healthcare and government, alongside several commercial sectors. This creates broad topical coverage but gives public search engines mixed audience signals because government and healthcare are not consistently framed as the primary market focus.

The homepage exposes separate service and industry URLs, a contact form, policy pages, and social links. In the visible extracted navigation, some industry labels appear to point to unrelated URL slugs, including **Startups** pointing to `/industries/transportation-logistics/` and **Government** pointing to `/industries/banks-insurance/`. This is a public-information-architecture inconsistency that warrants correction in the redesigned site’s canonical routes, internal linking, and redirect plan.

## Source 2 — Maximus public site

URL: https://maximus.com/  
Observed on: August 15, 2026

Maximus demonstrates a clear public-sector information architecture. Its homepage organizes entry points around **Who We Serve**, **What We Do**, news, and distinct market cards for defense, health services, and state and local government. Its public copy ties health services to concrete governmental outcomes such as program modernization, analytics, citizen services, SNAP accuracy, and veteran support. This is an observed messaging and navigation pattern only, not a claim about Maximus organic performance.

## Source 3 — LUKE public site

URL: https://lukestaffing.com/  
Observed on: August 15, 2026

LUKE frames its offer tightly around **healthcare staffing for U.S. government agencies**. Its visible page differentiates audience pathways by Department of Defense, Department of Homeland Security, and Department of Veterans Affairs, then connects each pathway to a job-search action. The site uses mission language, agency names, staffing context, and role-oriented navigation rather than a general IT-services taxonomy. This is a public content pattern worth analyzing, not a performance claim.

## Source 4 — Health Management Associates AI consulting page

URL: https://www.healthmanagement.com/services/information-technology-advisory-services/ai-consulting-services-for-healthcare/  
Observed on: August 15, 2026

This dedicated healthcare-AI service page uses a narrow intent match: it speaks directly to health and human services organizations, state and local agencies, Medicaid agencies, health plans, providers, and healthcare technology leaders. It breaks the offer into distinct modules such as AI procurement and vendor selection, governance and compliance, operational impact, and workforce readiness. It also names relevant operating frameworks including CMS health-IT requirements and the NIST AI Risk Management Framework. These observations show the level of contextual detail that a buyer- and search-focused EMFOI capability page should aim for; they do not establish rankings or traffic.

## Source 5 — EMFOI legacy crawl controls

URLs: https://www.emfoi.com/robots.txt and https://www.emfoi.com/sitemap_index.xml  
Observed on: August 15, 2026

The legacy robots file permits crawling and points to a Yoast sitemap index. The public sitemap index lists post, page, job-opening, category, tag, job-category, job-type, job-location, and author sitemaps. This indicates that a redesign launch must retain a deliberate XML sitemap and robots reference, while using canonical URLs and redirects to prevent legacy template URLs from becoming orphaned or duplicative. The public crawl resources cannot show which pages receive organic traffic or backlinks.

The redesigned homepage has a clear H1 focused on software, AI, and staffing for government and healthcare. ## Initial implications for EMFOI redesign

1. Preserve the redesign’s clear Government and Healthcare routes, and strengthen internal links between them and the specific capability pages that solve their needs.
2. Avoid general-purpose service labels as the primary SEO hierarchy. Each major page should state its target buyer, operating context, and delivery outcome in the opening heading and description.
3. Create controlled, factual subtopic coverage where EMFOI can support the claim, such as healthcare AI governance, public-sector digital delivery, and government healthcare workforce support. Each page should define the delivery scope and avoid unsubstantiated credentials, results, agency relationships, or regulatory claims.
4. Plan legacy-to-redesign redirects carefully for the public legacy service and industry URLs so relevance and visitors are not lost during migration.
